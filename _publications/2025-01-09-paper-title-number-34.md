The nuclear matrix, a proteinaceous gel composed of proteins and RNA, is 
an important nuclear structure that supports chromatin architecture, but 
its role in human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs) has not been described. 
Here we show that by disrupting heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoprotein 
U (HNRNPU) or the nuclear matrix protein, Matrin-3, primed hPSCs 
adopted features of the naive pluripotent state, including morphology 
and upregulation of naive-specifc marker genes. We demonstrate that 
HNRNPU depletion leads to increased chromatin accessibility, reduced DNA 
contacts and increased nuclear size. Mechanistically, HNRNPU acts as a 
transcriptional co-factor that anchors promoters of primed-specifc genes 
to the nuclear matrix with POLII to promote their expression and their RNA 
stability. Overall, HNRNPU promotes cell-type stability and when reduced 
promotes conversion to earlier embryonic states.
